Used Excavator Buying Checklist

10 inspection points before you buy a used excavator, loader or backhoe loader.

Used excavator buying checklist

Use this list when you inspect a used excavator, loader or backhoe loader. It is general guidance and does not replace a professional inspection.

  1. Documents and serial number. Match the plate with the paperwork. Ask for ownership documents and service records.
  2. Hour meter vs. wear. Compare hours with pedal, seat, track or tire and pin wear.
  3. Cold start. Start the engine cold. Check for hard starting, heavy smoke or unusual noise.
  4. Hydraulics. Look for leaks at cylinders, hoses and the pump. Operate every function under load.
  5. Undercarriage or tires. Check track chains, rollers, sprockets or tire wear and cracks.
  6. Pins and bushings. Check play at the boom, stick, bucket and linkage.
  7. Structure. Look for cracks or repaired welds on the frame, boom and stick.
  8. Cab and electronics. Test controls, gauges, lights, A/C and any warning lights.
  9. Fluids. Check engine oil, hydraulic oil and coolant for contamination.
  10. Payment safety. Do not pay in advance before verifying the machine and the seller.

Frequently asked questions

How many engine hours is too many for a used excavator?

There is no single number. Hours must be read together with maintenance records, working conditions and the actual wear on the machine. A well-maintained high-hour machine can be better than a neglected low-hour one.

What are the most important things to inspect?

Engine behavior (starting, smoke, noise), hydraulic leaks and cylinder condition, undercarriage or tire wear, pin and bushing play at the boom, stick and bucket, cab and controls, and the service history.

Should I test the machine under load?

Yes. Run all functions, including travel, swing and the boom, stick and bucket, and compare speed and smoothness. Weak or slow movement under load can indicate hydraulic problems.

Is an independent inspection worth it?

For higher-value machines, an independent inspector is a good investment because they can spot problems that are hard to see for a non-specialist.
